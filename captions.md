## Mon 19 Oct: Reward renewals

**LinkedIn**
Who keeps your family insured year after year?

Not the app. Not the call centre. Usually the agent who calls before the renewal date, chases the missing document, and sits with you when a claim gets stuck.

IRDAI's consultation paper caps individual health renewal commission at 10%. It already proposes renewal pay that rises with persistency, but only for life policies.

My ask to IRDAI (2 of 8): extend that idea to health. Agents who keep 80%+ of their health policies renewing should earn a higher renewal rate. Reward the service after the sale, not just the first sale.

Why it matters to you: a health policy that lapses can mean losing your waiting-period credit and starting again.

## Tue 20 Oct: Stop mis-selling, protect honest agents

**LinkedIn**
Mis-selling is real. Unfair business practice complaints against life insurers rose from 23,335 to 26,667 in FY25 (IRDAI Annual Report 2024-25).

IRDAI is right to act. But the fix should hit the source, not every agent alike. Three asks I'm taking to IRDAI:

4️⃣ Right of reply. No mis-selling record goes public before the agent sees the complaint and can respond. Many complaints start with claim rules the agent never controlled.
5️⃣ A clean-record badge. If the public registry can show mis-selling, let it also show agents with zero upheld complaints.
6️⃣ Target the source. Publish complaints by channel and put the strongest checks where the data points.

## Thu 22 Oct: Hospital bills, not agent commissions, drive your premium

**LinkedIn**
Hospital bills, not agent commissions, drive your health premium.

Medical inflation runs at an estimated 12–14% a year. Health premiums reached ₹1.37 lakh crore in FY26, up 15.6%. A private hospital room in Chennai can cost ₹16,000 a night, against ₹6,000 for a nearby 3-star hotel.

Now a compulsory 10% co-pay on retail health claims is reportedly being discussed from 1 January 2027. On a ₹5 lakh bill, that's ₹50,000 from your pocket. It is still only a proposal.

My view as an agent:
• Fix the cost first. Fair standard rates for common treatments, agreed by both hospitals and insurers, and no higher charges for insured patients (as Parliament's health committee recommended in August).
• Keep co-pay optional. If it ever comes, it must bring a lower premium or a no-claim rebate. (Thank you, Atul Shah, for raising this.)
• Your existing policy and your company's group cover are unchanged. Please don't drop or switch your cover because of the news.

I've added these points to my IRDAI comments.
